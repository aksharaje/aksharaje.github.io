  1. Language as Evidence: A Forensic Investigation of Silent Jurisdictional Assumptions in a Reasoning Model
    Akshara Raje · preprint, 2026 · pdf
    A language model asked a legal question in a language where no country is named still has to assume one. I investigate why Qwen3.6-27B tells English-speaking users its answer depends on where they live, whereas it answers the same questions in Chinese under mainland Chinese law without flagging the assumption. Using the model-forensics protocol, I read the model's reasoning to form hypotheses and test each with an intervention: rewriting Chinese questions in Traditional characters, forcing a casual style, resampling just before the model names a country. The behavior reflects an inference the model treats as settled rather than a failure to notice ambiguity.

    Hover a lane to focus. The hidden layer under the Chinese lane is what the model is quietly assuming.

  2. Position: Accountability Mechanisms Are Adopted Only When the Verifier Pays Nothing
    Akshara Raje · preprint, 2026 · pdf
    Proposals for accountability in agent-mediated work (AI use statements, detection, watermarking, content provenance) presuppose that the parties who would rely on them want them. I report a complete enumeration of 2,942 active job-posting queues on Upwork and Freelancer, hand-coded against a preregistered scheme and validated against injected positive controls. Requests for AI disclosure are near zero in either market. The mechanisms clients do adopt share a property the provenance stack lacks: each is free to the client, with its cost falling on the applicant. I argue cost incidence by whom pays to verify should be treated as a first-class design constraint, and that demand for accountability should be measured rather than assumed.

    Mechanisms whose cost falls on the applicant are routinely requested; those whose cost falls on the verifier are not.
